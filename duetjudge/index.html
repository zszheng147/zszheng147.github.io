TL;DR

Compare, don’t score.

DuetJudge listens to two clips conditioned on the same caption and answers A, B, or Tie — caption alignment first, perceptual quality second. Aggregating its pairwise probabilities gives a reinforcement-learning reward that improves Audex-2B, Audex-30B-A3B, and diffusion-based ETTA on all five automatic metrics and both human ratings.

Why

Judge the pair, not the clip.

A polished, upbeat pop arrangement is not a successful response to “a slow, mournful solo cello piece,” yet matching the requested instrument and mood does not make audible artifacts irrelevant. A useful judge must consider both, with an explicit priority: caption alignment first, perceptual quality second.

Problem

Pointwise scores never see the alternative

Existing evaluators score one clip at a time, even the ones trained on pairwise labels. A direct judge can inspect both clips and decide which audible differences matter under the caption.

Criterion

Make the priority explicit

Overall listening appeal need not coincide with request fulfillment, so DuetJudge learns a task-specific rule: a meaningful advantage in caption alignment decides, and perceptual quality separates comparably aligned candidates.

Payoff

A reward you can optimize against

The judge stays frozen while a generator is trained; comparisons among the generator’s own same-caption samples become the reward. Audex-2B, Audex-30B-A3B, and diffusion-based ETTA all improve.

Paper abstractFull text

Improving text-to-music models through reinforcement learning (RL) requires a reward model that can clearly quantify the quality of a text–music pair. In this domain, this usually includes (i) how high-quality the outputs sound, and (ii) how well they align with the provided text. We introduce DuetJudge, an on-policy pairwise reward model that learns to directly compare two instrumental pieces conditioned on the same caption, without assigning independent scalar scores to each. DuetJudge is functionally a ternary classifier that outputs either one piece wins or they are a tie. Particularly, DuetJudge prioritizes text–music alignment, and then considers perceptual quality of the music. DuetJudge achieves 71% agreement with human preferences on our internal test set, outperforming all existing score-based baselines. To use DuetJudge as a reward model in text-to-music RL, we (i) design a reward score based on aggregated pairwise preference probabilities, and (ii) introduce classifier-free guidance (CFG)-aware policy optimization for text-to-music models trained with CFG. We test DuetJudge using multiple in-distribution and out-of-distribution text-to-music models, and find systematic improvements in both automatic metrics and human ratings. Ablation studies further favor direct pairwise rewards over a Bradley–Terry scalar baseline under the same training settings. These results confirm the effectiveness of our proposed DuetJudge, our data curation and augmentation methods, our reward model training recipes, and our text-to-music RL training recipes.

02 · From judgments to rewards

Rewards from pairs

The judge never changes. For every caption the generator samples a group of clips, DuetJudge compares every pair in both orders, and each clip’s reward is how often it is preferred over its siblings. No reference recordings are needed.

From pairwise judgments to a reward
Order-averaged soft margins against every same-caption opponent
Soft, not hard. A win counts +1, a loss −1, a tie 0, weighted by the judge’s probabilities, so an unsure judge produces a small margin instead of a coin flip.
Both orders. Each pair is judged as (A, B) and (B, A); averaging removes position bias.
Relative, not absolute. Opponents are the policy’s own same-caption samples, so rewards sum to zero within a group and need no reference audio.

antisymmetric · each reward is a row mean · rewards sum to 0

CFG-aware policy optimization for Audex
Optimize the policy you sample from, and hold its baseline still

Audex samples with classifier-free guidance, so the guided policy is what GSPO updates and scores. The reward gradient returns through the conditional branch only, but both branches share one model: the null baseline still drifts and guidance amplifies the drift. Anchor both the guided policy and the null baseline to the frozen SFT reference.

Transfer to a diffusion generator: ETTA
Same judge, same reward; the generator learns from finished samples

ETTA’s audio never appears in the judge’s training data. Rewards become preference weights between 0 and 1; DiffusionNFT re-noises each finished sample, pulls the guided prediction toward it with weight w and pushes away with 1 − w, anchored to the frozen initial ETTA. Same frozen judge, same reward; only the update rule changes.

3.3

Keep the comparison pairwise

Under the same GSPO setup on Audex-2B, direct pairwise rewards beat a Bradley–Terry scalar reward model built from the same judgments (DuetJudge-BTRM) on all five automatic metrics. DPO on the judgment pairs helps too, but moving online with a scalar reward loses alignment.

Pairwise beats BTRM on all five metrics. Online alone is not enough: going from offline DPO to online GSPO with the scalar reward loses alignment.

Which parts of the recipe matter?Ablations

Every ablation lowers Audiobox-PQ, CMI-RM Musicality, CMI-RM Alignment, and MuQ: explicit tie labels, swap augmentation, order averaging, soft probability margins, and comparing against the policy’s own same-caption samples all contribute.
